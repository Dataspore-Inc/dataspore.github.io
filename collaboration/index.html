Problem Discovery & Operational Research

Where Does Your Business Data Break Down—and What Keeps You From Trusting AI?

Before building another dashboard or AI feature, we are mapping the real, unfiltered friction leaders face every week across HubSpot, Stripe, Zendesk, and Google Analytics.

This is a listening effort, not a sales pitch: Through our NSF-supported research initiative, we are interviewing CEOs, CFOs, and Directors of Operations, Customer Service, and Marketing to document which data mismatches and AI governance headaches hurt the most—and which ones are merely background noise.
Diagnostic by Seat

The Pains We Hear Most Across the Leadership Table

Every department feels the split between CRM, billing, support, and product usage differently. Some pains trigger board-level fire drills; others quietly drain hours every week.

CFO & Finance

Revenue & Churn Numbers That Never Match at Close

Acute Pain: Stripe invoices, HubSpot closed-won deals, and manual spreadsheets disagree on MRR, expansion, or net retention right before board meetings or audits.

Chronic Drag: Days spent exporting CSVs, matching customer IDs by hand, and defending which report is the "official" number—plus fear of AI tools hallucinating financial figures.

CEO & Executive Team

Conflicting Department Reports & Stalled AI Rollouts

Acute Pain: Sales, Support, and Finance walk into the weekly leadership meeting with three different versions of customer health and churn risk.

Chronic Drag: Pressure to adopt AI across the company while knowing your underlying data is too fragmented—and too exposed—to let AI assistants operate without constant supervision.

Director of Operations

Brittle Glue Code & Runaway AI Token Bills

Acute Pain: Point-to-point webhooks and zaps silently break when one tool updates a field, corrupting downstream reports without anyone noticing for weeks.

Chronic Drag: Early AI workflows either burn through API budgets feeding raw account records into prompts or fail because the AI lacks cross-tool context.

Director of Customer Service

Support Teams Flying Blind on Account Value & Usage

Acute Pain: High-value accounts silently stop logging in (visible in GA4) or have overdue billing issues (in Stripe) while Zendesk treats their tickets like routine inquiries.

Chronic Drag: Agents waste minutes tab-hopping across four systems to piece together a customer's history—and leadership cannot verify what AI support bots actually told customers.

Director of Marketing

Broken Attribution Between Spend, Product & Revenue

Acute Pain: Google Analytics shows campaign conversions, HubSpot tracks leads, and Stripe holds actual collected cash—making true CAC payback and LTV guesswork.

Chronic Drag: Trial users who turn into support headaches or quick churners look identical to great customers in top-of-funnel marketing reports.

AI & Data Governance

Zero Visibility Into What AI Tools See or Do

Acute Pain: Teams connect AI assistants to company tools without clear guardrails on sensitive customer records or an audit trail of what data was accessed.

Chronic Drag: Legal, finance, and security reviews stall promising AI projects because there is no clean checkpoint governing permissions and activity logs.

The "Minor" Pains That Compound

Small Daily Frictions We Are Testing in Our Research

Not every problem starts as an emergency. Often, small recurring annoyances quietly tax your best people every week:

Duplicate & Orphaned Customer Profiles

The same customer signs up with a personal email in GA4, pays under a billing email in Stripe, and opens tickets from a teammate's address in Zendesk.

"Quick Questions" That Take Two Days

Asking a simple cross-functional question—like "Which Q3 cohort had the highest ticket volume before renewing?"—turns into a multi-person spreadsheet project.

AI Answers You Have to Double-Check Anyway

If a team member still has to manually verify every AI-generated summary against Stripe and HubSpot, the AI hasn't actually saved any time.

Metric Definitions That Drift by Department

"Active customer," "churned account," and "qualified pipeline" mean slightly different things depending on which tool exported the chart.
